01
The position

Anyone can sell you panels. Almost nobody leaves the house better than they found it.

Our market sells a number on a spreadsheet and subcontracts the day that matters. Scaffold up for three weeks. A different crew each morning. Cable stapled across a gable because nobody was going to see it. The kit is a commodity — every installer buys from the same four distributors. The install isn't. That's the whole business, and it's the whole brand.

THE PROMISE, SHORT VERSION

The cable run. The loft. The driveway at five o'clock.

Brand architecture

One name. Two words after it.

BRIGHTWORK

The parent

Everything is BRIGHTWORK. The survey, the install, the invoice, the van, the person on the phone. There is no second logo anywhere in the business.

SUB-BRAND

BRIGHTWORK Care

Monitoring, servicing and the fix when something stops. A word, not a logo — same typeface, same weight, never re-drawn, never given its own colour.

SUB-BRAND

BRIGHTWORK Trade

What builders, developers and housing associations buy. Same standard, different paperwork. Again: a word after the name, nothing more.

One name. One promise. One standard. If a sub-brand ever needs its own mark, the answer is that it isn't a sub-brand — it's a different company.
03
What we photograph

Photograph the work, not the weather.

No blue-sky stock. No family laughing at a tablet. No drone shot that could be any roof in Britain. We shoot hands, kit, and the moment a job is being done properly — close enough that a tradesperson would nod at it.

08
Mark rules

Four rules. They're not negotiable.

01

CLEAR SPACE

The height of the W on all four sides. Nothing enters it — not a phone number, not an accreditation badge, not a rounded corner.

02

TWO TONES ONLY

Lime on WORK against black. Olive on WORK against ivory. Otherwise the whole mark in one flat colour. Never a gradient, never an outline.

03

NEVER DISTORT

Scale it. Don't condense it, arch it, tilt it, letterspace it or set it in another typeface because the sign-writer had one to hand.

04

ONE PER SURFACE

One mark on a van side. One on a chest. One on a page. A mark repeated three times reads as a company that isn't sure you noticed.

10
Type

One typeface, worked hard.

Aa Bb
0123

Inter, and nothing else. Black for anything that shouts, Regular for anything that explains. Headlines are tracked in tight and set at two or three times the size you'd expect — the size does the emphasis, so nothing needs italics, underlines or a second colour.

Numbers are the brand's favourite typography: kWp, kWh, dates, times, prices. Set them in tabular figures so a column of them lines up, and never round a number to make it prettier.

DISPLAY · 950 · -6.5% HEADLINE · 900 · -4.5% BODY · 400 · 1.45 MICRO · 800 · +14% FIGURES · TABULAR
11
Layout

Edge to edge, or don't bother.

01A rail, not a menu.
02One idea per screen.
03Nothing floats in the middle.

Content runs to the edge of the measure and stops hard. Sections are full-height and single-minded: a heading, one paragraph, one piece of evidence. If a page needs a third column to fit, it needs a second page. Rules and borders are hairlines — they divide, they don't decorate.

DESIGNED, NOT DECORATED

Photography

Four frames that sell the company.

The signal

One dot. One meaning.

LIVE

A lime dot means live: a system generating, a slot confirmed, a stage finished. It never means "new", "featured", "popular" or "on offer". One meaning is what makes it worth looking at.

Survey · Design · Scaffold · Install · Handover. The same five stages appear in the quote, in the app, in the emails and on the card left on the kitchen table. A customer should never have to ask where in the process they are.

The quote

A quote is a promise with a number on it.

ENQUIRY SURVEY QUOTE DESIGN SCAFFOLD INSTALL HANDOVER CARE
Two pages. The system, the working, the dates, the price. Nothing a solicitor wrote.

Every quote shows how the generation figure was reached — roof pitch, orientation, shading, the assumptions underneath it. If a customer can't check our maths, they're being asked to trust a stranger, and we'd rather they didn't have to. The price is one number with VAT already in it. No "from", no asterisk, no finance table dressed up as a discount.

How we operate

22
Proof

Two kinds, and we need both.

Proof you can see

Photographs of the work at the stages that matter, taken by the crew on the day and sent before we leave the street. The same set every job, so they're comparable — and so nobody has to decide what's worth photographing.

Proof you can check

Generation data from day one, the schedule we published against the dates we hit, and the neighbour three doors down who had it done last spring. Claims we can't evidence don't go in the quote.

23
The standard

What good work looks like, and what isn't ours.

  • The crew that surveyed it is the crew that fits it
  • Cable clipped at even spacing, labelled and dated
  • Driveway swept every day, not just the last one
  • Dates given once and kept
  • Bad news delivered first, with the fix attached
  • The customer can read their own generation figures
  • A different set of faces each morning
  • Scaffold left up because it suits the diary
  • "Up to", "from", and an asterisk
  • A price that changes after the survey
  • A loft left worse than it was found
  • A review asked for twice

Everything on the left costs money. That's the point — it's the part competitors won't copy.
