02

Keep recognition. Improve execution.

Evolution, not rebrand. PMB has years of recognition on vehicles, workwear, signage and online profiles, and that is an asset — not something to throw away.

Do

  • Retain the PMB name and basic visual DNA.
  • Redraw geometry and spacing properly.
  • Fix the relationship between PMB and Paving & Landscapes.
  • Produce horizontal, stacked, monogram and single-colour versions.
  • Produce van livery, embroidery, signage, favicon and avatar applications.

Do not

  • Gradients, gloss, bevels or drop shadows.
  • Fake stone texture inside the mark.
  • Celtic or Irish motifs.
  • A bright green because of the owner’s heritage.

The brand should feel relevant to the work, not themed around nationality.

The gold question — resolved in favour of the rules

The concept mockup sets the PMB wordmark large and in Warm Metal in both the header and the footer. That contradicts two rules in the same document: Warm Metal is “optional tiny accent only — never a dominant gold”, and the primary logo applications are Forest on Chalk, Chalk on Forest and one-colour.

A gold wordmark on a dark green nav is a 4.75:1 pair — legible, but it becomes the visual centre of the page and pulls the brand towards the generic premium-contractor look the positioning is trying to escape. brand.css therefore never sets the logo in metal. If PMB want the gold wordmark, that is a legitimate call — but it is a change to the colour rule, not an exception to it.

The lockup here is a stand-in

.pmb-logo sets the mark as live text in Manrope ExtraBold. When the redrawn artwork arrives it becomes the master, goes in a logo/ folder alongside these files, and the live-text version retires to favicon-sized fallbacks.

05

The ground, as tokens.

The interface is taken from what PMB builds. Each of these is a token in brand.css, so nothing is decoration for its own sake.

The motion signature: things settle

--pmb-settle is a short travel with a firm stop — compaction, not a spring. Nothing in PMB bounces, floats or drifts. Corners are softly rounded — 8px on buttons, inputs and tiles, 12px on larger panels — following the client’s approved mockup. The kerb line stays square-cut; that one is a drawn edge, not a container.

06

The work is the hero.

The site should look expensive because the projects look expensive — not because the interface is overloaded with decoration.

Shoot

  • Finished driveways, wide enough to show the whole property.
  • Before/after pairs — same angle, same lens, same light.
  • Low-angle material detail: joints, edging, aggregate, kerbs, steps, drainage.
  • Process: excavation, Type 1 sub-base, compaction, screeding, laying, finishing.
  • Patrick and the crew on real sites. Clean, not staged.
  • Twilight projects where lighting and landscaping do the work.
  • Drone only where it genuinely explains scale and layout.

Never

  • Oversaturated HDR.
  • Obvious AI architecture.
  • Stock photos of unrelated homes.
  • Fabricated customer imagery.

Natural colour, controlled contrast, deep greens, warm stone, realistic skies. The hero filter is deliberately light — enough to hold type over a driveway, not enough to flatten the stone.

Before/after is the most valuable asset PMB owns

Every completed job produces one for free, and it is the single best sales device a paving business has. Shoot it deliberately: mark the camera position before work starts, and return to it.

07

Transformation, not decoration.

Motion comes from what PMB physically does. Every major animation either explains construction, reveals a transformation, or makes the interface easier to use. If it does none of those, it doesn’t ship.

Layer 1 · GOOD WORK. UI motion

Page reveals, text entrances, counters, tickers, hover and tap states, nav transitions, card reveals, image masks, scroll choreography. This is the default.

Layer 2 · Material choreography

The same patterns, adapted to laying, stacking, revealing and transforming surfaces.

Layer 3 · Cinematic assets

Higgsfield or equivalent for the impossible shots — paving assembling, surfaces breaking away, aggregate transitions, ground-drop.

Layer 4 · Functional motion

Estimator progress, AI assistant state, upload confirmation, success states.

The brand guide requires the GOOD WORK. motion library as the primary animation system. Audited against its 170 components: eight of eleven mapped sections are covered, three need building. Full detail in MOTION-AUDIT.md.

Build the before/after scrub first

There is no image comparison component in the library — the nearest match, codecompare, is a source-code diff card. Build rule 4 says before/after is a primary interaction, not a buried gallery feature. Build it into the library rather than as a PMB one-off: every trades, renovation and restoration client will want it.

Reduced motion needs checking per snippet

Only 10 of the ~170 library components carry their own prefers-reduced-motion query. The library’s snippets are standalone by design — they bring their own <style> block, so the engine’s global off-switch does not reach them. Check each snippet’s static end state, not just that it stopped moving. “Still” and “usable” are different tests.

The same applies to counters: every one in the library loops on a setInterval because it has to demo itself in the gallery. Copy-pasting the preview code ships an infinitely counting trust strip.

Restraint

  • Floating blobs, tech particles or effects unrelated to paving.
  • Any animation that delays a quote, a call or an estimator step.
  • Animating everything — strong moments need quiet around them.

Always

  • Mobile gets simpler, faster versions of heavy sequences.
  • Tap works without hover.
  • The static experience is complete, not just still.
  • Real project imagery is the proof; cinematics are presentation.
08

Comfortable, premium, conversion-led.

Calm enough for a homeowner making a five-figure decision, and visibly more advanced than a standard contractor site.

Navigation

Compact and sticky. Transparent over the hero, deep forest on scroll. One clear CTA: Plan My Project.

Buttons

Solid Forest primary, outline Charcoal secondary. Minimum 44px touch height. A visible focus ring on every surface.

Cards

Minimal borders, softly rounded corners, large photography. No generic icon grids unless they genuinely aid scanning.

Forms

Large labels, plain English, one decision per step where possible.

Reviews

Large, specific and contextual — not a carousel of tiny quotes.

Texture

.pmb-grain at 0.045 opacity. Never over body copy.

Two things the mockup and the spec disagree about

Service count. The copy direction frames four services (Driveways · Patios · Landscaping · Restoration); the mockup shows five, adding Groundworks. Groundworks is real work and it sits under Landscaping in the architecture. Pick one and make the homepage, nav and IA agree.

Estimator length. The written spec is nine steps; the mockup shows five fields and a resolved range. Decide deliberately — a five-field estimator produces a range wide enough to be useless, a nine-field one loses people. A short qualifying set with progressive disclosure is the likely answer.

09

Straight-talking confidence.

PMB should sound like experienced people who know the work — not an agency writing about dream outdoor sanctuaries.

Good The finish matters. So does everything underneath it.
Weak We create stunning outdoor spaces tailored to your unique lifestyle.

Use

  • Specific materials and processes.
  • Short statements backed by proof.
  • Real locations and real projects.
  • Plain-English explanations of trade-offs.
  • Genuine customer language, accurately quoted and attributed.

Avoid

  • “Quality you can trust” with no evidence.
  • Unsupported superlatives — “best in Essex”.
  • Invented accreditations, guarantees, prices or quotes.
  • “Bespoke oasis”, “where dreams become reality”.
  • Daily SEO copy with no local or project evidence.

Reconfirm every one of these before launch

The current site states: £1m public liability insurance · five-year guarantee on all work · fully registered and fully insured · no deposit required, payment after completion and satisfaction · free written no-obligation quotations · seven-day cooling-off period.

These are the highest-liability content on the site. They appear in the hero trust strip, throughout the service pages, and in the AI assistant’s knowledge base — which means a stale claim gets repeated by a chatbot on demand. Get them confirmed in writing by PMB immediately before launch, and again whenever the knowledge base is refreshed.

The material and installer logos (Bradstone, Natural Paving, Marshalls, Hanson, Brett) need the same treatment: confirm what is a genuine approved-installer relationship and what is simply a material PMB buys. Only one of those can be presented as an accreditation.

11

Build rules.

The hand-off checklist. If the build drifts, come back here.

  1. Use the forest / limestone / chalk / charcoal palette consistently, from brand.css. Never a raw hex in a component.
  2. Use real PMB imagery wherever available. Label concept visuals clearly.
  3. The homepage communicates transformation + trust within the first screen.
  4. Before/after is a primary interaction, not a buried gallery feature.
  5. Motion comes from materials, construction and transformation.
  6. The estimator states plainly that pricing is indicative and subject to survey.
  7. The AI assistant answers from the PMB knowledge base and escalates uncertainty. It never invents a price, guarantee or accreditation.
  8. Every service page carries real proof: a project, a review, a process or a material detail.
  9. Location pages carry genuine local relevance, not keyword swaps.
  10. Mobile is first-class: large controls, fast load, reduced-motion support, clear tap targets.
  11. Reconfirm guarantee, insurance, no-deposit and payment terms, accreditations and any price claim with PMB before launch.
  12. Don’t copy the old site verbatim. Preserve the facts, rewrite the voice.

The final creative test

Does this feel like a 25+ year company with serious workmanship and serious proof — or does it feel like another paving template?

If it feels generic, it is not finished.
