# Beauty Heaven × Good Work — strategy session

Run of show for the deck at `/goodwork/brands/beauty-heaven-hub/strategy/`.
Presenter notes — kept out of `public/` deliberately, so they are never served
alongside the deck itself.

For Jess and Hollie. Roughly 45 minutes of presenting, and then the part that
actually matters.

**Read this first: the deck is not the meeting.** It is a spine for a
conversation you are trying to *start*, not a pitch you are trying to *close*.
There is no pricing in it and no commercial ask at the end. The last moment is
a question. Let it be a question.

## The shape

Five acts. The scope widens with each one, and the visual language cools from
Beauty Heaven's ivory and taupe towards the espresso and black of a technology
business. That drift is deliberate — by the time you are talking about a
marketplace, the room should already feel like a different kind of
conversation.

| Act | Moments | The move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Today** | 1–5 | What already exists. No proposal yet. |
| **01 Now** | 6–11 | Make the business you have considerably better. |
| **02 Owned** | 12–16 | Own more of what you depend on. |
| **03 Bigger** | 17–20 | Jess's idea, taken seriously. |
| **Decide** | 21–26 | Two paths, the honest caveats, the question. |

## Moment by moment

### Act I — Today (1–5)
Nothing is being sold here. The job is to prove you have looked properly.

**1 · Title.** Let it sit. Say almost nothing.

**2 · What you have already built.** Read the five back to them. This is the
foundation the whole deck stands on — everything after is *added to* a real
business, not a rescue of a broken one.

**3 · The ecosystem today.** The systems join up on screen as you talk. Do not
call anything bad. The line is *"none of these were chosen wrongly — they were
chosen one at a time."*

**4 · Keep · Improve · Connect · Replace · Own.** The most important slide in
Act I. Say out loud that most of the list is **keep** and **connect**. This is
where you kill the fear that you have come to replace everything.

**5 · Three opportunities.** The map that returns as every chapter break. Plant
it and move on — do not explain all three yet.

### Act II — 01 Now (6–11)
Concrete, near-term, obviously useful. Build credibility before you ask for
imagination.

**6 · One brand, one experience.**

**7 · The website.** Real screenshots of what is already live. Not a mockup —
say so.

**8 · The Content Console.** The first *concept*. Say the word. "We have built
this twice before, for two other businesses. This one would be taught Beauty
Heaven."

**9 · One idea, a whole campaign.**

**10 · The Concierge, and the phone.** ★ The emotional centre of Act II. Let
the conversation play out; do not talk over it. The nervous first message is
the point. Close on *"none of this makes Beauty Heaven less human — it makes
the team more available."*

**11 · "Our emails are shocking."** Their words, used with affection. Before
and after.

### Act III — 02 Owned (12–16)
The temperature drops. This is where the conversation becomes about ownership.

**12 · What sits underneath.**

**13 · Phorest: three routes.** ⚠ **Read the caveat on screen, out loud.** Do
not promise the data moves. The wording on the slide is the wording to use:
any future migration would prioritise preserving their historical customer and
operational data, *subject to confirming the available export routes, data
access and technical migration feasibility.* Say that Phorest is not the enemy
here — it is doing a job, and the question is only whether it is still the
right job.

**14 · Rent vs own.** Not "subscriptions are waste". The point is that some
things are worth renting forever and some are not, and nobody has ever sat
down and sorted theirs.

**15 · The Academy, owned.** Concept UI.

**16 · Only for Beauty Heaven?** ★ The hinge of the whole deck. Ask the
question and then **stop talking**. If they answer it themselves, Act IV
belongs to them rather than to you.

### Act IV — 03 Bigger (17–20)
Jess's idea. Handled as a serious proposition, not flattery.

**17 · "I want my brows done tonight."** Full bleed, no chrome. One line.
Silence.

**18 · The marketplace.** Four screens. Note the **Concept — not built** chip
on screen; point at it. The insight line at the bottom is the one worth
landing: *availability tonight is worth more than a five-star profile, and
nothing currently connects the two.*

**19 · More than a marketplace.** Fresha is named as context, deliberately.
It proves people pay for these tools. Whether *tonight* is a genuinely
different proposition is a research question — say "research question", not
"opportunity".

**20 · The ecosystem.** Eight beats; the circle closes and comes back into
education. Take it slowly — one beat per sentence. This is the biggest idea in
the room and it should take the longest to draw.

### Act V — Decide (21–26)
Land the plane. Widen nothing further.

**21 · Two possible futures.** They connect, but they are not the same project
and should not be funded, staffed or judged as one.

**22 · What we won't do.** ★ The credibility slide. Thirteen unknowns, several
of which could stop the project. Do not rush it — this is the moment that
separates you from a development agency looking for more development.

**23 · Prove it. Improve it. Scale it.** Start embarrassingly small. One town.

**24 · The actual conversation.** Ten questions. **Hand the room over here.**
If you are still talking five minutes into this slide, something has gone
wrong.

**25 · Our role, and what happens next.** Note the line about there being no
numbers in the presentation. Say it plainly: pricing something you have not
scoped would be guessing, and they would be right not to trust it.

**26 · What do you want to build next?** Ask it. Then be quiet.

## Things not to say

- Do not attack Phorest, the training platform, or anyone currently supplying
  them. Every one of those decisions was reasonable when it was made.
- Do not promise the Phorest data migrates. The slide's wording is the ceiling.
- Do not describe the marketplace as a certainty, a category winner, or an
  asset with a value. It is an idea with a research question attached.
- Do not put a number on any of it. If asked directly: *"I could give you one
  now and it would be wrong. Discovery first, then a proposal for whatever we
  agree is worth doing."*
- Do not call any of the concept UI "built". It is designed, not built, and
  every concept moment is labelled as such.
