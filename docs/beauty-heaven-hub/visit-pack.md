# Beauty Heaven Hub: visit pack

Everything for the day in one place. Print it. Nothing here is for Jess and Hollie except the message in section 1.

## 1. Message to send before the visit (edit and send)

> Hi Jess and Hollie, looking forward to seeing you on [day]. So we can get through it quickly and get the build moving, could you
> each do a few small things before I arrive? None of it takes long.
>
> 1. **Phone calls.** Ask your phone company for the call log for the last month (calls in, answered, missed, times), or if that's
>    awkward, ask whoever's on the desk to tally calls for a few days: answered, missed, and what each was about.
> 2. **Business papers.** Have your company number or a recent utility or bank statement in the business's name to hand, so we can
>    verify your business with Meta (Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp).
> 3. **One email.** You seem to have a few addresses. Decide which one is the real one for the business.
> 4. **A new phone number for the WhatsApp assistant.** A cheap new SIM or virtual number is fine. It must not already be on WhatsApp.
> 5. **Phorest.** Have your account number ready, and know who has the manager login. If you can, run last year's sales-by-person
>    and bookings-by-source reports and bring them.
> 6. **Questions clients ask.** Jot down the ten things people ask most on the phone and on WhatsApp (prices, aftercare, what to bring,
>    parking, how long it takes).
> 7. **Your team.** Ask them quickly if they're happy to be on the new website, with a photo and a couple of lines.
> 8. **Photos and video.** Point me to where your best ones live (phone, Instagram, a folder).
>
> **Please don't send any passwords.** We'll set everything up together on the day, from inside each account, and you stay in control.
> Thanks! [name]

## 3. Talking about the Academy, honestly
You don't need to seem naive. You are genuinely new to how the Academy works, so ask like it:
- "Walk me through what happens when someone wants a course, from the first message to their last day, so the website explains it properly."
- "When someone finishes, what do they get, and what does it say on it?" (Ask it as a website question. Note the answer exactly. Don't react.)
- "Which courses are running right now, and are the dates up?"
- **The closing question, once:** "One last thing, and it's up to you. Down the line, would you want to talk about whether marketing the Academy would be worth doing? No rush, I want to understand how it works first. If yes, I'll show you exactly what it would cost and include, with every number on the table."
Don't discuss accreditation as a problem, don't mention rebuilding the courses, and don't put a price on anything on the day.

## 5. What to look at while you are there
- The front desk phone and how a booking is taken and entered.
- The Phorest reports (sales by staff, booking source, no-shows, lapsed clients, opted-in count).
- A client card in Phorest: does it hold photos or forms?
- The consultation app (screen only, no client data).
- The salon's insurance certificate on the wall or in a folder.
- Where the photos and videos live now, who took them, and whether the practitioners are happy for the salon to use the ones they took. Ask who is allowed to move things into `Approved`.

## 6. Rules for the day
- Don't quote a price or a fee change. Don't raise practitioner fees or memberships. Wait for the results.
- Don't give medical, legal, tax or employment advice. "I'll come back to you on that."
- Don't screenshot or photograph client names, addresses or photos.
- Write down exactly what they say, especially where Jess and Hollie disagree.
